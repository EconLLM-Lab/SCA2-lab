 
  When a method conditioning a language model on a culture preserves an external benchmark, what has been shown about survey respondents? We study one method. Adapters are compiled from six declared country-level signs of a preference benchmark, through synthetic preference pairs, and read survey items without naming the country. Training installs the synthetic labels on unseen pairs, and on trust the adapters keep the benchmark's order of countries across a new scenario bank. A prompt stating the same signs does too, so the signs carry it. The dimensions are entangled. A preregistered screen of held-out items, against a retrospective criterion, yields a weak signal that registered contrasts cannot separate from questionnaire readers. The adapters' order of countries does not detectably agree with respondents, and their answer distributions lie farther from the survey than the base model's; a country-named prompt lies closer. The instrument can complement human pretesting; pre-piloting remains untested.
 

   Keywords:  synthetic respondents; large language models; measurement validity; survey item screening; preregistration; cross-national measurement.
